Brand guidelines

Download the files ↓

One person, one name, one mark. This page is for anyone who puts them on something: a conference slide, a partner page, a post, a credit line. It says what the mark is, which colours it uses, how the name is written, and what not to do. Everything on it can be downloaded below.

The mark

Four horizontal bars, solid at the left, breaking into particles toward the right. Read left to right it is a broadcast becoming data: a match, a feed, a season turned into the points a chart or a model works with. That is the work, sports media, digital and AI, in one gesture. It is also the solid present and the uncertain future, the edge where I have always worked: what we know on the left, what is still forming on the right.

The four colours come from the scarf in the photo on the home page. The scarf is an accident that stuck; the mark is not about it. The bars always run left to right, always in this order from the top: sand, turquoise, cobalt, red.

Versions

Four files cover every use. The tile is for browser tabs, app icons and avatars. The wide mark sits next to the name in a masthead or a credit line. The bare mark is the tile without its background, for a page that already has a dark or white ground. The stacked lockup puts the mark over the name, for squares, posts and slides.

  • Tile

    Favicon, app icon, avatar. Ink background, 7 px corner on 32. Works on light and dark tab bars.

    agwas-mark-tile.svg
  • Tile, paper

    The same on white, for places that want a light tile.

    agwas-mark-tile-paper.svg
  • Wide mark

    Next to the name at 20 to 28 px tall, or large on its own. The particles have room to dissolve.

    agwas-mark-wide.svg
  • Stacked lockup

    Mark over the name, name set as outlines. For a square, a post, a slide. Dark and with-person versions below.

    agwas-lockup-stacked.svg
  • Bare mark

    No tile. On white or on a dark ground, never on a photograph or a colour.

    agwas-mark.svg
  • Touch icon

    Square, no rounding: iOS rounds it. Export to 180 px PNG.

    agwas-touch-icon.svg

Size and space

16 · 32 · 64 · 128 px
Smallest tile
16 px. The bars still read; the particles become a frayed edge, which is fine.
Smallest wide mark
20 px tall. Below that, use the tile.
Clear space
One bar height on every side. Nothing else sits inside it.
Next to the name
Mark first, then the name, on one baseline, a gap of half the mark's height.
Stacked or side by side
Side by side in mastheads and credit lines. Stacked where the space is square or the name must be large: posts, slides, the end of a video.

With the photo

For a banner, the mark and the name sit on the left and the photo on the right. The photo is the hero shot from the home page, cropped so that Carlo stands at the right edge, and it fades into the ground behind the name instead of meeting it at an edge. On white the room's grey-blue disappears into the paper; on night the fade is longer.

Two rules. The mark never sits on the photo itself; it stays on the plain ground to the left of the fade. And the line under the name is fixed: Carlo De Marchis · sports media, digital, AI.

Colour

The four colours belong to the mark and to data. Text is ink on paper, or paper on night in the apps. Cobalt is the only one of the four that holds as a link colour on both grounds; the other three never carry text.

The name

A Guy With A Scarf · Carlo De Marchis

A Guy With A Scarf. Title case, every word, always complete. In a byline or a masthead it is followed by the person: A Guy With A Scarf · Carlo De Marchis, with the name in mute.

AGWAS is a file name and a way of talking, not a public mark. Never "a guy with a scarf" in lower case, never "Guy with a Scarf", never "AGWAS" in a title.

Type

Helvetica Neue 300Helvetica Neue 400Helvetica Neue 500Helvetica Neue 700

Helvetica Neue, with Helvetica and Arial as fallbacks. The name is set in weight 500. Headings are 300 or 400 with tight letter spacing; body text is 400. The sport apps keep their own faces inside; the name and the mark look the same everywhere.

Do not

  • Mirror the mark or run the particles right to left.
  • Recolour the bars, drop one, or reorder them.
  • Stretch the tile or the wide mark; scale them as a block.
  • Put the bare mark on a photograph, a gradient or a colour.
  • Add a scarf, a letter or a sport pictogram to it.
  • Set the name in lower case, in a serif, or as an acronym.
